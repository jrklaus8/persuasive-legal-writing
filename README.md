# Persuasive Legal Writing

A structured skill for legal writing that actually moves people — judges, tribunals, opposing counsel, clients. The skill distils techniques from Justice Elena Kagan's opinions and dissents, the Supreme Court advocacy of David Boies and Ted Olson, Ross Guberman's analytical framework, and Bryan A. Garner's plain-language method (This skills is based on Larissa Meredith-Flister's published skill at Lawve.ai: https://lawve.ai/en/skills/persuasive-legal-writing-larissa-meredith-flister + added Garner's philosophy and teachings).

## What This Is

A single `SKILL.md` file designed to be loaded into an AI writing session — via Claude Code, Codex, or any system that supports skill/instruction files. When active, it instructs the model to apply four layers of technique to any legal document it drafts or revises.

It is not a style guide to read once and forget. It is an operational instruction set for AI-assisted legal writing.

## The Four Parts

### Part One: Architecture

How to structure and sequence arguments so the reader reaches your conclusion before you have fully argued for it.

- **Syllogistic opening** — issue, opposing position, defeating principle, all in two or three sentences (Kagan's model in *Florida v. Harris*)
- **Binary opening** — force the reader to choose between two starkly different characterisations of the dispute (Boies & Olson's model in *Perry v. Schwarzenegger*)
- **Sequence by persuasive force** — strongest argument first; second argument as independent confirmation; end with a forward-looking consequence
- **Conversational signposting** — "Begin with...", "Turn next to...", "Now just put them together" (Kagan in *Kloeckner*)
- **Parting thought** — the last substantive paragraph reframes the case at its highest level of generality, not "for the foregoing reasons..."

### Part Two: Craft

How to write the sentences and paragraphs that carry the structure.

- **Razor-sharp clarity** — one job per sentence; short for conclusions, longer for reasoning; syllogism that makes the conclusion feel inevitable
- **Vivid, concrete examples** — narrative hypotheticals (Kagan's binoculars in *Jardines*), recruited-intuition analogies, parades of horribles
- **Parallel construction** — structural tool, not decoration; antithesis to expose double standards
- **Strategic quoting** — merge snippets into your own sentences; quote the opponent's words back; reserve block quotes for genuinely devastating passages
- **Human voice** — concrete Anglo-Saxon words over abstract Latinate ones; direct address to the reader; dialogue with the reader through question-and-answer

### Part Three: Avoiding AI-Sounding Legal Prose

A detailed catalogue of structural and phrase-level tells that make legal writing sound machine-generated, with before-and-after examples.

Structural tells: announced structure ("the argument is therefore limited in four respects"), meta-commentary ("the contrast deserves to be worked through"), standalone axioms ("if X, then Y fails"), the "Nor... It does, however..." negation scaffold, pedantic throat-clearing before definitions.

Phrase-level tells: narrating your own language choices, forced idioms ("earns its keep"), the "narrower concern" retreat, evaluative grading of concepts ("that is a useful diagnostic idea"), vague attribution ("some accounts assume"), hollow axioms ("it is not a free-standing fairness charter"), self-assessing your own argument's ambition ("the right use of section 229 here is modest").

Self-referential tells: the document talking about itself ("this paper"), situating itself temporally ("for a July 2026 conference paper"), disclaiming its own analogies, standalone scoping paragraphs that exist only to acknowledge limits.

The underlying rule: **if a sentence is about the document rather than about the subject, cut it.**

### Part Four: The Garner Plain-Language Method

Bryan A. Garner's ten principles applied as a revision discipline.

1. **Clarity over complexity** — "pursuant to the aforementioned circumstances" → "under these facts"
2. **Good thinking before drafting** — unclear writing reflects unclear thinking; editing is intellectual work
3. **Every word must earn its place** — "null and void" → "void"; "each and every" → "each"; target 20–30% reduction
4. **Structure over decoration** — headings, topic sentences, logical flow beat fancy sentences
5. **Human voice** — active voice as default; sentence-length variation; formal without being archaic
6. **Editing discipline** — read aloud; cut 30%; delete the first sentence of every paragraph and see if it's stronger
7. **Typography and formatting** — white space, headings as complete arguments, no substantive footnotes
8. **Example-driven teaching** — every abstract rule followed immediately by a concrete application
9. **Authority with readability** — classical rhetoric and usage scholarship modernised for real readers
10. **Ethical communication** — clarity is a professional duty; legal writing affects rights, liberty, and access to justice

## The 10-Step Revision Checklist

1. **Opening** — frame or summarise?
2. **Sequence** — strongest argument first; consequence at the end?
3. **Examples** — every abstract principle grounded in at least one concrete example?
4. **Parallelism** — lists grammatically parallel; contrasts structured as antithesis?
5. **Quotations** — snippets merged into your own sentences, not dropped as block quotes?
6. **Voice** — does it sound like a person or an institution?
7. **Ending** — does it reframe the case, or start with "for the foregoing reasons"?
8. **Repetition audit** — no unintentional tic across paragraphs?
9. **Garner pass** — cut 20–30%, active voice, strong verbs, no throat-clearing, topic sentences that guide?
10. **AI-prose audit** — no structural or phrase-level tells from Part Three?

## Usage

Load `SKILL.md` into your AI session and ask it to draft, edit, or review any legal document. The skill triggers on phrases like:

> "make it more persuasive" / "sharpen this" / "strengthen the argument" / "write like a top advocate" / "plain language" / "cut the legalese" / "Garner style" / "rewrite this section"

It is designed to complement voice and tone skills (which handle personal register) by adding the persuasion technique, plain-language, and AI-avoidance layers on top.
